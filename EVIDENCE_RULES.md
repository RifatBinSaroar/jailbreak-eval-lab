# Evidence Rules

The research must distinguish clearly between what a source demonstrates, what we infer, and what remains unknown.

## For every paper we rely on

Record, where available:

- direct paper link, DOI, arXiv ID, or venue
- research problem
- benchmark / dataset
- domain and subdomains
- exact success definition
- validator / judge
- labels or score
- whether evaluation is semantic, static, functional, behavioural, or hybrid
- whether code is executed
- human ground truth or manual validation
- metrics
- known false positives or false negatives
- limitations acknowledged by the authors
- our interpretation
- novelty threat to this project
- open questions

## Claim discipline

Use three explicit buckets:

1. **Paper demonstrates** — directly supported by the source.
2. **Our interpretation** — a reasoned reading that goes beyond direct wording.
3. **Open question** — not yet established.

## Gap discipline

Do not write “no prior work exists” unless a systematic search supports that statement.

Preferred language while evidence is incomplete:

- “We have not yet found…”
- “The closest work identified so far is…”
- “A possible remaining gap is…”
- “This novelty claim remains under review.”

## Website rule

The public website should never display a literature-derived claim without a traceable source record.
